📖 Quick Glossary & Basic Definitions

Essential Core Concepts & Key Definitions • Unit V: Coordination Compounds (JKBOSE / CBSE Official Syllabus - 07 Marks)

1. Coordination Compound: A molecular compound in which a central metal atom or ion is permanently bound to a fixed number of surrounding neutral molecules or anions (ligands) by coordinate covalent (dative) bonds, retaining its identity in solid as well as in aqueous solution.
2. Ligand & Denticity: An electron-pair donor atom, ion, or molecule bound to the central metal ion (Lewis base). Denticity is the number of donor atoms through which a single ligand binds to the metal (unidentate, didentate, hexadentate).
3. Coordination Number (CN): The total number of coordinate σ-bonds formed between the central metal atom/ion and donor atoms of the surrounding ligands (most commonly CN = 4 or 6).
4. Chelate Effect: The enhanced thermodynamic stability of coordination complexes formed by polydentate (chelating) ligands (which form stable 5- or 6-membered rings with the metal) compared to similar complexes formed by unidentate ligands, driven primarily by an increase in entropy (ΔS > 0).
5. Crystal Field Splitting (Δo / Δt): The energy separation between the split d-orbital energy levels (t2g and eg in octahedral fields; e and t2 in tetrahedral fields) caused by the electrostatic repulsion of surrounding ligand point charges.
6. Spectrochemical Series: An experimentally determined arrangement of common ligands in the increasing order of their crystal field splitting ability (Δ): I- < Br- < Cl- < F- < OH- < H2O < NH3 < en < NO2- < CN- < CO.

1. Double Salts vs Coordination Complexes & Core Terminology

1. Double Salts vs Coordination Complexes
• Double Salts: Molecular addition compounds that exist only in solid state and completely dissociate into their simple constituent ions in aqueous solution (e.g. Carnallite: KCl • MgCl2 • 6H2O; Mohr's Salt: FeSO4 • (NH4)2SO4 • 6H2O; Potash Alum: K2SO4 • Al2(SO4)3 • 24H2O). They give positive chemical tests for all individual ions.

• Coordination Complexes: Retain their complex entity ([MLn]) intact in aqueous solution and do NOT dissociate into individual metal and ligand ions (e.g. K4[Fe(CN)6] gives 4K+ and [Fe(CN)6]4-, but gives NO test for Fe2+ or CN- ions).
2. Types of Ligands based on Denticity & Binding Mode
• Unidentate: Binds via 1 donor atom (e.g. Cl-, H2O, NH3, CN-, OH-).
• Didentate: Binds via 2 donor atoms simultaneously (e.g. Oxalate ion (ox): C2O42-; Ethane-1,2-diamine (en): NH2-CH2-CH2-NH2).
• Polydentate / Hexadentate: Binds via 6 donor atoms (e.g. EDTA4-: Ethylenediaminetetraacetate ion, 2 N + 4 O donors).
• Ambidentate Ligands: Unidentate ligands possessing 2 different donor atoms but binding through only one at a time (e.g. -NO2- nitro vs -ONO- nitrito; -SCN- thiocyanato vs -NCS- isothiocyanato).
• Homoleptic vs Heteroleptic: Homoleptic complexes have only 1 type of ligand (e.g. [Co(NH3)6]3+); Heteroleptic complexes have > 1 type of ligand (e.g. [Co(NH3)4Cl2]+).

2. IUPAC Nomenclature of Mononuclear Coordination Compounds

📌 DEFINITION: IUPAC Rules for Naming Coordination Complexes
1. Order of Naming: In ionic complexes, the Cation is named first, followed by the Anion.
2. Naming the Coordination Entity: Within the coordination sphere, Ligands are named first in alphabetical order, followed by the central metal.
3. Ligand Names:
• Anionic ligands end in -o: Chlorido (Cl-), Cyanido (CN-), Oxalato (C2O42-), Hydroxido (OH-).
• Neutral ligands retain special names: Ammine (NH3), Aqua (H2O), Carbonyl (CO), Nitrosyl (NO).
• Greek prefixes (di, tri, tetra, penta, hexa) indicate number. For chelating/complex ligands with numerical prefixes, use bis (2), tris (3), tetrakis (4).
4. Metal Name Ending:
• If the complex sphere is Cationic or Neutral: Metal retains its normal English name (e.g. Chromium, Cobalt, Platinum).
• If the complex sphere is Anionic: Metal name ends in -ate (e.g. Ferrate [Fe], Cuprate [Cu], Cobaltate [Co], Platinate [Pt], Argentate [Ag], Aurate [Au]).
5. Oxidation State: Indicated by a Roman numeral in parentheses immediately following the metal name without space (e.g. Cobalt(III)).
📝 WORKED BOARD IUPAC & HYBRIDISATION EXAMPLE: Master IUPAC Naming Examples
Problem: Write IUPAC names for: (i) [Co(NH3)5(CO3)]Cl, (ii) K3[Fe(C2O4)3], (iii) [Pt(NH3)2Cl(NO2)], (iv) [Cr(en)3]Cl3.
Step-by-Step Solution:
1. [Co(NH3)5(CO3)]Cl: Pentaamminecarbonatocobalt(III) chloride.
2. K3[Fe(C2O4)3]: Potassium trioxalatoferrate(III).
3. [Pt(NH3)2Cl(NO2)]: Diamminechloridonitrito-N-platinum(II).
4. [Cr(en)3]Cl3: Tris(ethane-1,2-diamine)chromium(III) chloride.

3. Isomerism in Coordination Compounds

1. Structural Isomerism (4 Types)
• Ionisation Isomerism: Exchange of an ion inside coordination sphere with counter ion outside (e.g. [Co(NH3)5SO4]Br gives precipitate with AgNO3; [Co(NH3)5Br]SO4 gives precipitate with BaCl2).
• Solvate / Hydrate Isomerism: Difference in whether water molecules act as ligands or as water of crystallization (e.g. [Cr(H2O)6]Cl3 violet vs [Cr(H2O)5Cl]Cl2 • H2O grey-green vs [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]Cl • 2H2O dark green).
• Linkage Isomerism: Arises when ambidentate ligands coordinate through different donor atoms (e.g. [Co(NH3)5(NO2)]Cl2 yellow nitro vs [Co(NH3)5(ONO)]Cl2 red nitrito).
• Coordination Isomerism: Exchange of ligands between cationic and anionic complex entities (e.g. [Co(NH3)6][Cr(CN)6] and [Cr(NH3)6][Co(CN)6]).
2. Stereoisomerism (Geometrical & Optical)
• Geometrical Isomerism:
• Square Planar [MA2B2]: cis-[Pt(NH3)2Cl2] (Cisplatin, anti-cancer drug) vs trans-[Pt(NH3)2Cl2].
• Octahedral [MA4B2]: cis-[Co(NH3)4Cl2]+ (violet) vs trans-[Co(NH3)4Cl2]+ (green).
• Octahedral [MA3B3]: Facial (fac) (3 identical ligands occupy adjacent corner positions of one octahedral face) vs Meridional (mer) (ligands occupy positions around a meridian plane), e.g. [Co(NH3)3(NO2)3].

• Optical Isomerism (Chirality): Non-superimposable mirror images (enantiomers: dextro d and laevo l) that rotate plane-polarized light in opposite directions (e.g. d- and l-[Co(en)3]3+).
Note: Tetrahedral complexes [MABCD] are chiral, while square planar complexes never show optical isomerism because they possess a plane of symmetry.

4. Theories of Bonding: Werner, VBT & Crystal Field Theory (CFT)

(i) Werner's Coordination Theory (1893)

📌 DEFINITION: Werner's Theory Postulates
1. Primary Valency: Ionisable, non-directional, corresponds to Oxidation State of the metal, and is satisfied exclusively by negative ions.
2. Secondary Valency: Non-ionisable, directional in space, corresponds to Coordination Number, satisfied by neutral molecules or negative ions, and determines the 3D spatial geometry (octahedral, tetrahedral, square planar).

• Classic AgNO3 Precipitation Evidence:
• CoCl3 • 6NH3 ⇒ [Co(NH3)6]Cl3: Precipitates 3 moles of AgCl.
• CoCl3 • 5NH3 ⇒ [Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2: Precipitates 2 moles of AgCl.
• CoCl3 • 4NH3 ⇒ [Co(NH3)4Cl2]Cl: Precipitates 1 mole of AgCl.

(ii) Valence Bond Theory (VBT) & Hybridisation

Inner vs Outer Orbital Complexes
• Inner Orbital (Low-Spin) Complexes (d2sp3): Strong field ligands (CN-, NH3, CO) force pairing of (n-1)d electrons, freeing two 3d orbitals for hybridisation (e.g. [Co(NH3)6]3+: d2sp3, diamagnetic).
• Outer Orbital (High-Spin) Complexes (sp3d2): Weak field ligands (F-, Cl-, H2O) cannot cause electron pairing, so outer 4d orbitals are used (e.g. [CoF6]3-: sp3d2, paramagnetic with 4 unpaired electrons).
• Square Planar vs Tetrahedral: [Ni(CN)4]2- is dsp2 (square planar, diamagnetic); [NiCl4]2- is sp3 (tetrahedral, paramagnetic with 2 unpaired electrons).

(iii) Crystal Field Theory (CFT) & d-Orbital Splitting

📐 THEORY & ELECTRONIC CONFIGURATION: Crystal Field Splitting in Octahedral & Tetrahedral Fields
In a free gaseous transition metal ion, all five d-orbitals (dxy, dyz, dxz, dx2-y2, dz2) are degenerate (same energy).

1. Octahedral Splitting (Δo):
Ligands approach along the Cartesian axes (x, y, z). Orbitals pointing directly along the axes (dx2-y2, dz2) experience greater electrostatic repulsion and rise to higher energy eg set (+0.6 Δo). Orbitals pointing between axes (dxy, dyz, dxz) experience less repulsion and drop to lower energy t2g set (-0.4 Δo).

2. Tetrahedral Splitting (Δt):
Ligands approach between the axes. Hence, the splitting is inverted and smaller:
Δt = 4 9 Δo
Because Δt is always less than Pairing Energy (P), tetrahedral complexes are exclusively high-spin complexes!

3. Electron Pairing Criterion in Octahedral Complexes (d4 System):
• Weak Field Ligands (Δo < P): High-spin complex ⇒ configuration is t2g3 eg1 (4 unpaired electrons).
• Strong Field Ligands (Δo > P): Low-spin complex ⇒ configuration is t2g4 eg0 (2 unpaired electrons).

5. Importance & Applications of Coordination Compounds

1. Analytical Chemistry & Qualitative Analysis: Identification of cations relies on colored complexes: Ni2+ with dimethylglyoxime (DMG) forms bright red [Ni(DMG)2]; Fe3+ with SCN- forms blood-red [Fe(SCN)]2+; Cu2+ with NH3 forms deep blue [Cu(NH3)4]2+. Water hardness is estimated by titration with EDTA.
2. Metallurgy & Extraction of Metals: MacArthur-Forrest Cyanide Process: Gold and Silver extracted via soluble cyano-complexes: 4Au + 8NaCN + 2H2O + O2 → 4Na[Au(CN)2] + 4NaOH. Zinc displaces pure gold: 2[Au(CN)2]- + Zn → [Zn(CN)4]2- + 2Au ↓. Mond Process: Nickel purified via volatile [Ni(CO)4].
3. Biological Systems: • Chlorophyll: Magnesium (Mg) coordination complex (photosynthesis).
• Hemoglobin: Iron (Fe2+) porphyrin complex (oxygen carrier).
• Vitamin B12 (Cyanocobalamin): Cobalt (Co) complex (prevents pernicious anemia).
4. Medical Applications & Chelation Therapy: • Cisplatin (cis-[Pt(NH3)2Cl2]): Potent chemotherapy drug inhibiting cancer tumor growth.
• EDTA (Ca-EDTA complex): Used in chelation therapy to remove toxic Lead (Pb) poisoning from bloodstream.
• D-Penicillamine: Chelates and excretes excess Copper in Wilson's disease.

6. Master Revision Formula & Rules Cheat Sheet

1. Coordination Rules & Equations: • Effective Atomic Number (EAN): EAN = Z - (Oxidation State) + 2(Coordination Number).
• Spin-Only Magnetic Moment: μs = √[n(n+2)] BM.
• Tetrahedral vs Octahedral Splitting: Δt = (4/9) Δo.

2. Hybridisation & Geometries: • sp3: Tetrahedral ([NiCl4]2-, [Ni(CO)4], μ = 2.83 BM for Ni2+).
• dsp2: Square Planar ([Ni(CN)4]2-, [Pt(NH3)2Cl2], diamagnetic μ = 0).
• d2sp3: Inner Orbital Octahedral ([Co(NH3)6]3+, [Fe(CN)6]3-).
• sp3d2: Outer Orbital Octahedral ([CoF6]3-, [Fe(H2O)6]2+).

3. Spectrochemical Series Summary: I- < Br- < S2- < SCN- < Cl- < N3- < F- < OH- < C2O42- < H2O < NCS- < EDTA4- < NH3 < en < NO2- < CN- < CO.
