📖 Quick Glossary & Basic Definitions

Essential Core Concepts & Key Definitions • Unit IV: d- and f-Block Elements (JKBOSE / CBSE Official Syllabus - 07 Marks)

1. Transition Elements (d-Block): Elements having partially filled (incompletely filled) (n-1)d subshell in their elementary ground state or in any one of their common stable oxidation states. General valence configuration: (n-1)d1-10 ns1-2.
2. Non-Typical Transition Elements (Zn, Cd, Hg): Zinc, Cadmium, and Mercury have completely filled d10 configurations in their elemental state as well as in their common +2 oxidation states. Hence, they are d-block elements but not regarded as typical transition elements.
3. Lanthanoid (Lanthanide) Contraction: The steady and progressive decrease in atomic and ionic radii (M3+) of lanthanoid elements with increasing atomic number (from 57La to 71Lu), caused by the poor shielding effect of 4f electrons.
4. Spin-Only Magnetic Moment (μs): The magnetic moment arising purely from unpaired electron spins: μs = √[n(n + 2)] Bohr Magnetons (BM), where n is the number of unpaired d-electrons.
5. Interstitial Compounds: Non-stoichiometric compounds formed when small non-metallic atoms (such as H, C, N, B) are trapped inside the interstitial voids of the transition metal crystal lattice (e.g. TiC, Fe3H, VN).
6. Inner-Transition Elements (f-Block): Elements in which the differentiating electrons enter the antepenultimate (n-2)f subshell. Consists of Lanthanoid (Lanthanide)s (Lanthanides) (4f series) and Actinoid (Actinide)s (Actinides) (5f series).

1. General Characteristics & Electronic Configuration of d-Block Elements

The d-block elements occupy Groups 3 to 12 in the middle of the Periodic Table, bridging the electropositive s-block alkali/alkaline earth metals and electronegative p-block elements. They are called transition elements because their properties represent a gradual transition from ionic to covalent character.

(i) General Valence Electronic Configuration

📌 DEFINITION: Electronic Configuration of d-Block
General Configuration: (n-1)d1-10 ns1-2 (where n = 4, 5, 6, 7).

• Anomalous Configurations in 3d Series:
• Chromium (24Cr): [Ar] 3d5 4s1 (Half-filled extra stability of 3d5 + higher exchange energy).
• Copper (29Cu): [Ar] 3d10 4s1 (Completely-filled extra stability of 3d10 + symmetrical charge distribution).

2. General Trends in Properties of First Row (3d) Transition Metals

1. Metallic Character & High Enthalpy of Atomization
All transition elements are hard, ductile metals with high tensile strength, high melting/boiling points, and high enthalpies of atomization.

• Origin: Due to the presence of unpaired (n-1)d electrons, transition metals form strong interatomic covalent bonds alongside metallic bonding. Enthalpy of atomization reaches a maximum in the middle of each series (e.g. V, Cr) where unpaired d-electrons are highest.
2. Atomic & Ionic Radii Trends (Sc → Zn)
• Initial Decrease (Sc to Cr): Effective nuclear charge (Zeff) increases steadily while shielding by d-electrons is poor, pulling electron clouds closer.
• Mid-Series Constancy (Fe, Co, Ni): Increasing nuclear charge is almost exactly balanced by increasing interelectronic repulsion among added d-electrons.
• Slight Increase at End (Cu, Zn): Completely filled 3d10 subshell experiences strong electron-electron repulsive forces, slightly expanding atomic radius.
3. Variable Oxidation States
Transition metals exhibit multiple variable oxidation states because the energy difference between (n-1)d and ns orbitals is very small, allowing electrons from both orbitals to participate in bond formation.

• Highest Oxidation State in 3d Series: Manganese (+7 in KMnO4) utilizing all 5 3d-electrons and 2 4s-electrons.
• Most Common Oxidation State: +2 across 3d series (achieved by loss of 4s2 electrons). Sc shows only +3 ([Ar] core); Zn shows only +2 (3d10 core).
4. Magnetic Properties & Spin-Only Formula
Most transition metal ions and complexes are paramagnetic (attracted by magnetic field) due to unpaired d-electrons.
μs = √[n(n + 2)] BM
where n = number of unpaired electrons and 1 BM (Bohr Magneton) = 9.27 × 10-24 J T-1.
Species with 0 unpaired electrons (Sc3+, Ti4+, Zn2+, Cu+) are diamagnetic (μ = 0).
5. Color of Transition Metal Ions (d-d Transitions)
In the presence of surrounding ligands, degenerate d-orbitals split into two sets (t2g and eg). When white light falls on the complex, an electron absorbs energy corresponding to visible light and jumps from lower to higher d-orbital (d-d transition). The transmitted complementary wavelength produces the observed color.

• Colorless Ions: Ions with d0 (Sc3+, Ti4+) or d10 (Zn2+, Cu+) are completely colorless because no d-d transitions are possible.
6. Catalytic Properties
Transition metals and their compounds act as industrial catalysts (e.g. finely divided Fe in Haber process, V2O5 in Contact process, Ni in hydrogenation) due to:
1. Variable Oxidation States: Easily form unstable reaction intermediates.
2. Vacant d-orbitals & Large Surface Area: Adsorb reactant molecules, weakening their chemical bonds.
7. Interstitial Compounds & Alloy Formation
• Interstitial Compounds: Extremely hard, have very high melting points (higher than pure metals), retain metallic conductivity, and are chemically inert.
• Alloy Formation: Because transition metals have similar atomic radii (within 15% Hume-Rothery rule), atoms of one metal can readily substitute for atoms of another metal in the crystal lattice (e.g. Brass Cu-Zn, Bronze Cu-Sn, Stainless Steel Fe-Cr-Ni).
📝 WORKED BOARD CONCEPTUAL & NUMERICAL EXAMPLE: Spin-Only Magnetic Moment Calculation
Problem: Calculate the spin-only magnetic moment of Mn2+ (Z = 25) and Fe2+ (Z = 26) ions in aqueous solution.
Step-by-Step Solution:
Step 1 (For Mn2+): Ground state Mn: [Ar] 3d5 4s2 ⇒ Mn2+: [Ar] 3d5.
Number of unpaired electrons n = 5.
μs = √[5(5 + 2)] = √35 = 5.92 BM.
Step 2 (For Fe2+): Ground state Fe: [Ar] 3d6 4s2 ⇒ Fe2+: [Ar] 3d6.
Number of unpaired electrons n = 4.
μs = √[4(4 + 2)] = √24 = 4.90 BM.

3. Potassium Dichromate (K2Cr2O7): Preparation & Properties

(i) Industrial Preparation from Chromite Ore (FeCr2O4)

📐 CHEMICAL EQUATION & REACTION MECHANISM: 3-Step Industrial Synthesis of K2Cr2O7
Step 1: Roasting Chromite Ore with Sodium Carbonate:
4FeCr2O4 + 8Na2CO3 + 7O2 → 8Na2CrO4 (Yellow) + 2Fe2O3 + 8CO2

Step 2: Acidification of Sodium Chromate to Sodium Dichromate:
2Na2CrO4 + H2SO4 → Na2Cr2O7 (Orange) + Na2SO4 + H2O

Step 3: Potassium Substitution (KCl Crystallization):
Because K2Cr2O7 is much less soluble in cold water than Na2Cr2O7, it easily crystallizes out:
Na2Cr2O7 + 2KCl → K2Cr2O7 ↓ (Bright Orange Crystals) + 2NaCl

(ii) Chromate-Dichromate pH Equilibrium

📌 DEFINITION: pH Dependent Interconversion
In aqueous solution, yellow chromate (CrO42-) and orange dichromate (Cr2O72-) exist in a dynamic pH-dependent equilibrium (Oxidation state of Cr remains +6 in both):
2CrO42- (Yellow) + 2H+ ⇌ Cr2O72- (Orange) + H2O
Cr2O72- (Orange) + 2OH- ⇌ 2CrO42- (Yellow) + H2O
• In Acidic Medium (pH < 7): Orange Cr2O72- predominates.
• In Basic Medium (pH > 7): Yellow CrO42- predominates.

(iii) Powerful Oxidizing Properties of K2Cr2O7 in Acidic Medium

In acidic solution, dichromate acts as a strong oxidizing agent (Cr2O72- → 2Cr3+, E° = +1.33 V):

Cr2O72- + 14H+ + 6e- → 2Cr3+ (Green) + 7H2O
  • • Oxidizes Iodide to Iodine: 6I- + Cr2O72- + 14H+ → 3I2 + 2Cr3+ + 7H2O
  • • Oxidizes Ferrous to Ferric: 6Fe2+ + Cr2O72- + 14H+ → 6Fe3+ + 2Cr3+ + 7H2O
  • • Oxidizes H2S to Sulfur: 3H2S + Cr2O72- + 8H+ → 3S ↓ + 2Cr3+ + 7H2O
  • • Oxidizes Stannous to Stannic: 3Sn2+ + Cr2O72- + 14H+ → 3Sn4+ + 2Cr3+ + 7H2O

4. Potassium Permanganate (KMnO4): Preparation & Properties

(i) Industrial Preparation from Pyrolusite Ore (MnO2)

📐 CHEMICAL EQUATION & REACTION MECHANISM: 2-Step Industrial Synthesis of KMnO4
Step 1: Alkaline Oxidative Fusion of Pyrolusite with KOH and KNO3/O2:
2MnO2 + 4KOH + O2 → 2K2MnO4 (Dark Green Manganate) + 2H2O

Step 2: Electrolytic Oxidation or Acidic Disproportionation:
• Electrolytic Oxidation: MnO42- → MnO4- (Intense Purple Permanganate) + e-
• Chemical Disproportionation: 3MnO42- + 4H+ → 2MnO4- (Purple) + MnO2 ↓ + 2H2O

(ii) Oxidizing Actions in Different Media

1. In Acidic Medium (5e- transfer, E° = +1.51 V):
MnO4- + 8H+ + 5e- → Mn2+ (Nearly Colorless) + 4H2O
• Fe2+ → Fe3+  |  I- → I2  |  C2O42- (Oxalate) → 2CO2  |  S2- → S  |  NO2- → NO3-
2. In Neutral or Faintly Alkaline Medium (3e- transfer, E° = +1.69 V):
MnO4- + 2H2O + 3e- → MnO2 (Brown precipitate) + 4OH-
• Iodide is oxidized to Iodate (IO3-): 2MnO4- + H2O + I- → 2MnO2 + 2OH- + IO3-
• Thiosulfate is oxidized to Sulfate: 8MnO4- + 3S2O32- + H2O → 8MnO2 + 6SO42- + 2OH-

5. Lanthanoid (Lanthanide)s (Lanthanides) (4f Series): Electronic Structure & Lanthanoid (Lanthanide) Contraction

(i) General Electronic Configuration & Oxidation States

The 14 elements from Cerium (58Ce) to Lutetium (71Lu) following Lanthanum (57La) constitute the Lanthanoid (Lanthanide) series.

  • • General Valence Electronic Configuration: [Xe] 4f1-14 5d0-1 6s2.
  • • Principal Stable Oxidation State: +3 (formed by losing two 6s and one 5d/4f electron).
  • • Unusual Oxidation States (+2 and +4):
    • Ce4+ (4f0) & Tb4+ (4f7): Extra stability of empty/half-filled 4f subshells. Ce4+ is a powerful analytical oxidizing agent.
    • Eu2+ (4f7) & Yb2+ (4f14): Extra stability of half-filled/fully-filled 4f subshells. Eu2+ is a strong reducing agent.

(ii) Lanthanoid (Lanthanide) Contraction & Its 4 Major Consequences

📌 DEFINITION: Lanthanoid (Lanthanide) Contraction
Lanthanoid (Lanthanide) Contraction: The steady and continuous decrease in atomic and ionic radii of lanthanoid elements with increasing atomic number across the series from La3+ (106 pm) to Lu3+ (85 pm).

• Physical Cause: 4f orbitals have highly diffused spatial shapes and exert an extremely poor shielding effect on outer electrons. Consequently, as the nuclear charge increases by 1 unit at each successive element, the effective nuclear charge (Zeff) increases progressively, pulling the entire electron cloud inward.
1. Identical Radii of 4d and 5d Transition Pairs (Chemical Twins): Because lanthanoid contraction cancels the expected increase in size down a group, 4d and 5d elements of the same group exhibit almost identical radii (e.g. Zr = 160 pm, Hf = 159 pm; Nb ≈ Ta; Mo ≈ W) and possess virtually indistinguishable chemical properties.
2. Difficulty in Separation of Lanthanoid (Lanthanide)s (Lanthanides): Because their ionic radii (M3+) and chemical properties are nearly identical, separating pure individual lanthanoids from ores requires advanced ion-exchange chromatography.
3. Decrease in Basic Strength of Hydroxides: As ionic size decreases from La3+ to Lu3+, the covalent character of M-OH bond increases according to Fajan's rules. Hence, La(OH)3 is the most basic, and Lu(OH)3 is the least basic.

6. Actinoid (Actinide)s (Actinides) (5f Series) & Lanthanoid (Lanthanide)s (Lanthanides) vs Actinoid (Actinide)s (Actinides) Comparison

(i) General Characteristics of Actinoid (Actinide)s (Actinides)

  • • Electronic Configuration: [Rn] 5f1-14 6d0-1 7s2 (from 90Th to 103Lr).
  • • Variable Oxidation States: Actinoid (Actinide)s (Actinides) exhibit a much broader range of oxidation states (+3, +4, +5, +6, +7, e.g. NpO22+, UO22+) because the energy difference between 5f, 6d, and 7s orbitals is very small.
  • • Radioactivity: All actinoids are radioactive; elements beyond Uranium (Z > 92) are man-made synthetic transuranic elements with short half-lives.
  • • Actinoid (Actinide) Contraction: 5f electrons have even poorer shielding than 4f electrons, so actinoid contraction from element to element is greater than lanthanoid contraction.

(ii) Master Comparison Cards: Lanthanoid (Lanthanide)s (Lanthanides) vs Actinoid (Actinide)s (Actinides)

🪙 1. Lanthanoid (Lanthanide)s (Lanthanides) (4f Series)
• Oxidation States: Predominantly +3; only few elements show +2 or +4.
• Radioactivity: Non-radioactive (except Promethium, 61Pm).
• Oxo-cations: Do not form oxo-cations.
• Complex Formation: Lower tendency to form coordination complexes.
• Basicity: Compounds are less basic compared to actinoid analogs.
⚡ 2. Actinoid (Actinide)s (Actinides) (5f Series)
• Oxidation States: Exhibit higher and broader oxidation states (+3, +4, +5, +6, +7).
• Radioactivity: All actinoids are highly radioactive.
• Oxo-cations: Form stable oxo-cations (e.g. UO22+, PuO22+, UO+).
• Complex Formation: Great tendency to form complexes due to smaller ionic size and higher charge.
• Basicity: Hydroxides are more basic than lanthanoid hydroxides.

7. Master Revision Formula & Chemical Cheat Sheet

1. Electronic Configurations & Magnetic Moment: • General d-Block: (n-1)d1-10 ns1-2.
• General Lanthanoid (Lanthanide)s (Lanthanides): [Xe] 4f1-14 5d0-1 6s2.
• General Actinoid (Actinide)s (Actinides): [Rn] 5f1-14 6d0-1 7s2.
• Spin-Only Magnetic Moment: μs = √[n(n+2)] BM.

2. Potassium Dichromate (K2Cr2O7) Reactions: • pH Equilibrium: 2CrO42- + 2H+ ⇌ Cr2O72- + H2O.
• Acidic Oxidizing Half-Reaction: Cr2O72- + 14H+ + 6e- → 2Cr3+ + 7H2O (E° = +1.33 V).

3. Potassium Permanganate (KMnO4) Reactions: • Acidic Medium: MnO4- + 8H+ + 5e- → Mn2+ + 4H2O (E° = +1.51 V).
• Neutral / Alkaline: MnO4- + 2H2O + 3e- → MnO2 + 4OH- (I- → IO3-).

4. Lanthanoid (Lanthanide) Contraction Pairs: • Identical Size Pairs: Zr ≈ Hf, Nb ≈ Ta, Mo ≈ W.
• Basicity Order: La(OH)3 > Ce(OH)3 > ... > Lu(OH)3.
