📖 Quick Glossary & Basic Definitions

Key Fundamental Terms & Concepts for Chapter 1: Diversity in Living World (Zoology)

1. Animal Kingdom: Multicellular, eukaryotic, heterotrophic organisms lacking cell walls and chlorophyll.
2. Radial Symmetry: Body symmetry where any plane passing through central axis divides body into two equal halves (Cnidaria, Ctenophora).
3. Bilateral Symmetry: Body symmetry where body can be divided into identical left and right halves in only one plane (Platyhelminthes to Chordata).
4. Diploblastic & Triploblastic: Embryos with 2 germ layers (Ectoderm + Endoderm) or 3 germ layers (Ectoderm + Mesoderm + Endoderm).
5. Coelom: Mesoderm-lined fluid-filled body cavity located between body wall and alimentary canal.
6. Pseudocoelom: Body cavity not lined by mesoderm; mesoderm exists as scattered pouches (Aschelminthes).
7. Notochord: Mesodermally derived flexible supporting rod located on dorsal side during embryonic development.
8. Non-Chordates: Animals lacking a notochord throughout life cycle (Porifera to Hemichordata).
9. Chordates: Animals possessing notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, and paired pharyngeal gill slits.
10. In-Situ Conservation: Protection and management of endangered species within their natural habitats (National Parks, Sanctuaries, Biosphere Reserves).
11. National Park: Strictly protected ecosystem reserve under Wildlife Protection Act where human activity, grazing, and forestry are completely prohibited.
12. Dachigam National Park: Famous national park in Srinagar (J&K) providing exclusive refuge to critically endangered Hangul (Kashmir Stag).
13. Hemis High Altitude NP: India's largest national park located in Ladakh, famous as Snow Leopard Capital of the World.
14. Kazinag National Park: LOC border national park in Baramulla (J&K) protecting the endangered Markhor (world's largest wild goat).
15. Salim Ali National Park: Wetland national park in Srinagar named after Dr. Salim Ali (Birdman of India).
16. Wildlife Sanctuary: Protected area reserved for specific fauna conservation where limited human activities (grazing, timber collection) are permitted.
17. Biosphere Reserve: Large international land/coastal ecosystem under UNESCO MAB program integrating biodiversity conservation with sustainable human development.
18. Core, Buffer & Transition Zones: 3 functional concentric divisions of Biosphere Reserves balancing strict protection, research, and human cooperation.

Animal Kingdom; General characters and classification of animals (non-chordates up to phyla level and chordates up to the level of class).

The Animal Kingdom (Metazoa) encompasses immense structural and functional diversity comprising over 1.5 million described species. Animals are classified based on fundamental body architectural parameters including Levels of Organization, Body Symmetry, Embryonic Germ Layers, Coelom (Body Cavity), Segmentation (Metamerism), and Notochord Presence.

(i) Basis of Animal Classification: Levels of Organization, Symmetry, Germ Layers, Coelom & Segmentation

• Levels of Organization:
- Cellular Level: Loose cell aggregates with minimal functional division of labor (Phylum Porifera).
- Tissue Level: Cells performing same function group into tissues (Phyla Cnidaria and Ctenophora).
- Organ Level: Tissues group into specialized organs (Phylum Platyhelminthes).
- Organ-System Level: Organs cooperate into complex digestive, circulatory, excretory, and neural systems (Phyla Aschelminthes to Chordata).

• Coelom (Body Cavity) Classification:
- Acoelomates: Body cavity completely absent; space filled with mesodermal parenchyma tissue (Platyhelminthes).
- Pseudocoelomates: Body cavity present but not lined by mesoderm; mesoderm exists as scattered pouches between ectoderm and endoderm (Aschelminthes).
- Eucoelomates (True Coelomates): Fluid-filled body cavity completely lined by mesodermal peritoneum (Annelida to Chordata).

• Embryonic Germ Layers:
- Diploblastic Animals: Cells arranged in two embryonic layers: outer Ectoderm and inner Endoderm with an intervening undifferentiated jelly-like layer called Mesoglea (Cnidaria, Ctenophora).
- Triploblastic Animals: Developing embryo possesses three germ layers: Ectoderm, Mesoderm, and Endoderm (Platyhelminthes to Chordata).

• Body Symmetry:
- Asymmetrical: Any plane passing through center does not divide body into equal halves (Sponges).
- Radial Symmetry: Any central longitudinal plane divides body into identical halves (Cnidarians, Ctenophores, Adult Echinoderms).
- Bilateral Symmetry: Body can be divided into identical left & right halves in only one vertical plane (Platyhelminthes to Chordates).

(ii) Non-Chordate Phyla (Porifera to Hemichordata): General Characters & Examples

• 1. Phylum Porifera (Sponges): Aquatic, cellular level, asymmetrical. Feature Water Transport / Canal System (Ostia ➔ Spongocoel ➔ Osculum) lined by flagellated Choanocytes (Collar Cells). Skeleton made of spicules or spongin fibers. Reproduction hermaphrodite. Examples: Sycon (Scypha), Spongilla (Freshwater sponge), Euspongia (Bath sponge).

{{FIG_1_4}}

• 2. Phylum Cnidaria (Coelenterata): Aquatic, tissue level, radial symmetry, diploblastic. Feature stinging cells called Cnidoblasts / Cnidocytes containing nematocysts for anchorage, defense, and prey capture. Exhibit two body forms: sessile Polyp (Hydra, Adamsia) and free-swimming Medusa (Aurelia / Jellyfish). Show Metagenesis (alternation of generations) in Obelia. Examples: Physalia (Portuguese man-of-war), Meandrina (Brain coral).

{{FIG_1_5}}

• 3. Phylum Ctenophora (Sea Walnuts / Comb Jellies): Exclusively marine, tissue level, radial symmetry. Feature 8 external rows of ciliated Comb Plates for locomotion and exhibit Bioluminescence. Examples: Pleurobrachia, Ctenoplana.

• 4. Phylum Platyhelminthes (Flatworms): Dorso-ventrally flattened body, organ level, bilateral symmetry, triploblastic, acoelomate. Feature specialized Flame Cells for osmoregulation and excretion. Possess hooks and suckers in parasitic forms. Examples: Taenia (Tapeworm), Fasciola (Liver fluke).

{{FIG_1_6}}

• 5. Phylum Aschelminthes (Roundworms): Circular cross-section, organ-system level, bilateral symmetry, triploblastic, Pseudocoelomate. Complete digestive tract with muscular pharynx. Sexes dioecious (females longer than males). Examples: Ascaris (Roundworm), Wuchereria (Filaria worm), Ancylostoma (Hookworm).

{{FIG_1_7}}

• 6. Phylum Annelida (Segmented Worms): Bilateral symmetry, triploblastic, coelomate, exhibit true Metameric Segmentation. Excretion via Nephridia. Closed circulatory system. Locomotion via setae or lateral parapodia (in Nereis). Examples: Nereis, Pheretima (Earthworm), Hirudinaria (Blood-sucking leech).

{{FIG_1_8}}

• 7. Phylum Arthropoda (Jointed Appendages): Largest Phylum in animal kingdom (2/3rd of all species). Organ-system, bilateral, triploblastic, coelomate with chitinous exoskeleton. Body divided into Head, Thorax, and Abdomen. Respiration via gills, book gills, book lungs, or tracheal system. Excretion via Malpighian Tubules. Open circulatory system. Examples: Apis (Honey bee), Bombyx (Silkworm), Laccifer (Lac insect), Anopheles, Locusta (Pest), Limulus (Living fossil King crab).

{{FIG_1_9}}

• 8. Phylum Mollusca (Soft-bodied Animals): Second Largest Phylum. Unsegmented soft body covered by calcareous shell. Body divided into Head, Muscular Foot, and Visceral Hump. Soft spongy skin mantle layer over visceral hump forms mantle cavity containing feather-like gills (ctenidia). Feeding organ Radula (file-like rasping organ). Examples: Pila (Apple snail), Pinctada (Pearl oyster), Sepia (Cuttlefish), Loligo (Squid), Octopus (Devil fish), Aplysia (Sea hare), Dentalium (Tusk shell), Chaetopleura (Chiton).

• 9. Phylum Echinodermata (Spiny-skinned Animals): Exclusively marine, organ-system, triploblastic, coelomate. Adult radial symmetry; Larva bilateral symmetry. Feature unique Water Vascular System for locomotion, food capture, and respiration. Excretory system absent. Examples: Asterias (Starfish), Echinus (Sea urchin), Antedon (Sea lily), Cucumaria (Sea cucumber), Ophiura (Brittle star).

{{FIG_1_10}}

• 10. Phylum Hemichordata (Worm-like Animals): Small marine worm-like group. Body divided into anterior Proboscis, Collar, and long Trunk. Possess rudimentary buccal diverticulum called Stomochord. Respiration via gills; excretion by Proboscis Gland. Examples: Balanoglossus, Saccoglossus.

{{FIG_1_11}}

(iii) Phylum Chordata Architecture: Subphyla & Classes of Vertebrata

• Phylum Chordata Fundamental Features: Characterized by 4 primary hallmarks: 1. Notochord, 2. Dorsal hollow nerve cord, 3. Paired pharyngeal gill slits, 4. Post-anal tail. Divided into 3 Subphyla: Urochordata (notochord in larval tail), Cephalochordata (notochord extends head to tail), and Vertebrata (notochord replaced by cartilaginous/bony Vertebral Column).

{{FIG_1_12}}

• 7 Classes of Subphylum Vertebrata:
1. Class Cyclostomata (Jawless Vertebrates): Ectoparasites on fishes. Suctorial circular mouth without jaws. 6-15 pairs of gill slits. Cartilaginous cranium & vertebral column. Marine but migrate to freshwater for spawning (anadromous migration); larvae undergo metamorphosis and return to ocean. Examples: Petromyzon (Lamprey), Myxine (Hagfish).
2. Class Chondrichthyes (Cartilaginous Fishes): Marine, streamlined body with cartilaginous endoskeleton. Notochord persistent throughout life. Mouth ventral. Tough skin covered with microscopic Placoid Scales. Gills separate without operculum (cover). No air bladder (must swim constantly to avoid sinking). Pelvic fins bear Claspers in males. Poikilothermous (cold-blooded). Examples: Scoliodon (Dog fish), Pristis (Saw fish), Carcharodon (Great white shark), Trygon (Sting ray), Torpedo (Electric ray).
3. Class Osteichthyes (Bony Fishes): Marine and freshwater fishes with bony endoskeleton. Mouth terminal. Skin covered with Cycloid / Ctenoid Scales. 4 pairs of gills covered by an Operculum on each side. Air bladder present regulating buoyancy. 2-chambered heart. Examples: Marine: Exocoetus (Flying fish), Hippocampus (Sea horse); Freshwater: Labeo (Rohu), Catla (Katla), Clarias (Magur); Aquarium: Betta (Fighting fish), Pterophyllum (Angel fish).
4. Class Amphibia: Adapted to both aquatic and terrestrial habitats. Body divided into Head and Trunk. Moist skin without scales. Eyes have eyelids; Tympanum represents ear. Alimentary canal, urinary, and reproductive tracts open into common chamber called Cloaca. 3-chambered heart (2 auricles, 1 ventricle). Poikilotherms. Examples: Bufo (Toad), Rana (Frog), Hyla (Tree frog), Salamandra (Salamander), Ichthyophis (Limbless amphibia).
5. Class Reptilia: Terrestrial animals characterized by creeping/crawling locomotion. Body covered by dry non-glandular skin with epidermal scales or scutes. Tympanum represents ear. 3-chambered heart (4-chambered in Crocodiles). Poikilotherms. Snakes and lizards shed scales as skin cast. Examples: Chelone (Turtle), Testudo (Tortoise), Kamaleon (Chameleon), Calotes (Garden lizard), Crocodilus (Crocodile), Alligator, Hemidactylus (Wall lizard); Poisonous snakes: Naja (Cobra), Bangarus (Krait), Vipera (Viper).
6. Class Aves (Birds): Presence of Feathers, capability of flight (except flightless birds like Ostrich). Forelimbs modified into wings. Jaws modified into toothless beak. Long hollow bones filled with air cavities (Pneumatic Bones). Digestive tract has additional chambers: Crop and Gizzard. 4-chambered heart. Homeothermic (Warm-blooded). Respiration by lungs supplemented by air sacs. Examples: Corvus (Crow), Columba (Pigeon), Psittacula (Parrot), Struthio (Ostrich), Pavo (Peacock), Aptenodytes (Penguin), Neophron (Vulture).
7. Class Mammalia: Possess milk-producing Mammary Glands to nourish young ones. Presence of hair on skin, external ears (Pinnae), and heterodont teeth in jaws. 4-chambered heart. Homeothermic. Respiration by lungs. Viviparous (except oviparous Platypus). Examples: Oviparous: Ornithorhynchus (Platypus); Viviparous: Macropus (Kangaroo), Pteropus (Flying fox), Camelus (Camel), Macaca (Monkey), Rattus (Rat), Canis (Dog), Felis (Cat), Elephas (Elephant), Equus (Horse), Delphinus (Dolphin), Balaenoptera (Blue whale), Panthera tigris (Tiger), Panthera leo (Lion).

National Parks with special reference to Dachigam, Kishtwar, Salim Ali, Kazinag and Hemis.

National Parks represent premier strictly protected in-situ conservation areas designated under Section 35 of the Wildlife Protection Act (1972). National parks protect entire natural ecosystems, flora, fauna, landscape, and geomorphological features where all human exploitation, livestock grazing, and private land rights are completely prohibited.

(i) Dachigam National Park: Location, Topography & Conservation of Hangul (Kashmir Stag)

• Dachigam National Park Profile:
- Location & Area: Located 22 km from Srinagar city in Jammu & Kashmir, covering an area of 141 sq km in the Zabarwan Range of the Western Himalayas.
- Etymology: "Dachigam" literally translates to "Ten Villages", commemorating the ten local villages relocated during its establishment as a protected catchment area for Harwan reservoir supplying drinking water to Srinagar.
- Topographical Division: Divided into Lower Dachigam (un-glaciated lower valley) and Upper Dachigam (high alpine crags, alpine pastures, and Marsar Lake).

• Hangul (Kashmir Stag - Cervus hanglu hanglu) Conservation:
- Dachigam National Park is the LAST WORLDWIDE REFUGE for the critically endangered Hangul (Red Deer subspecies endemic to Kashmir valley).
- Habitat Preference: Hangul undergoes seasonal altitudinal migration: spending summers in alpine pastures of Upper Dachigam and descending to oak/coniferous forests of Lower Dachigam in winter.
- Threats & Management: Habitat fragmentation, poaching, predation of fawns by leopards/dogs, and domestic sheep competition led to Project Hangul monitoring and intensive habitat protection.

(ii) Kishtwar National Park, Salim Ali National Park & Kazinag National Park

• 1. Kishtwar High Altitude National Park:
- Location: Located in Kishtwar district of J&K, spread over 400 sq km in high alpine mountain catchments of Chenab river.
- Biodiversity: Features high coniferous forests (Deodar, Spruce, Pine) and alpine meadows. Habitat for Himalayan Brown Bear, Himalayan Black Bear, Himalayan Musk Deer, Ibex, Snow Leopard, and rare birds like Himalayan Monal and Koklass Pheasant.

• 2. Salim Ali National Park (City Forest NP):
- Location: Located in Srinagar city (covering 9 sq km) adjacent to Dal Lake.
- History & Named After: Named after eminent Indian ornithologist Dr. Salim Ali ("Birdman of India").
- Ecological Significance: Freshwater wetland and riparian forest habitat supporting resident and migratory waterfowl (Pintails, Mallards), Hangul deer, Serow, and Himalayan Snowcock.

• 3. Kazinag National Park:
- Location: Located in Baramulla district of J&K along the Line of Control (LOC) border.
- Flagship Species: Created primarily to conserve the critically endangered Markhor (Capra falconeri), the world's largest wild goat species featuring striking corkscrew-shaped horns.
- Consolidated Reserves: Established by merging Limber Wildlife Sanctuary, Lachipora Wildlife Sanctuary, and Naganari conservation reserve.

(iii) Hemis High Altitude National Park (Ladakh): Cold Desert & Snow Leopard Conservation

• Hemis National Park Profile:
- Location & Extent: Located in eastern Ladakh region north of the Himalayas, spanning a massive 4,400 sq km, making it the LARGEST NATIONAL PARK IN INDIA.
- Ecosystem Type: High-altitude trans-Himalayan cold desert ecosystem characterized by barren mountains, sparse alpine vegetation, and deep rocky gorges.

• Snow Leopard (Panthera uncia / Uncia uncia) Capital:
- Hemis is globally famous as the "Snow Leopard Capital of the World", supporting the highest density of Snow Leopards in any protected area in South Asia.
- Associated High-Altitude Fauna: Bharal (Blue Sheep), Shapu (Ladakh Urial), Asiatic Ibex, Tibetan Wolf, Eurasian Brown Bear, Tibetan Argali, Golden Eagle, and Lammergeier Vulture.

Concept of Sanctuaries and Biosphere reserves.

(i) Wildlife Sanctuaries: Definition, Legal Status & In-Situ Conservation vs National Parks

• Wildlife Sanctuaries Definition: A Wildlife Sanctuary is a legally declared protected area created under the Wildlife Protection Act (1972) specifically for the protection and preservation of target wild animal species (fauna).

• Key Differences: Wildlife Sanctuary vs National Park:
- Focus: Sanctuaries are species-oriented (e.g. sanctuary for wild asses or migratory birds); National Parks protect the entire ecosystem (flora, fauna, geomorphology).
- Human Interference: Sanctuaries permit limited human activities such as private land ownership, cattle grazing, and timber harvesting (provided wildlife is not harmed); National Parks prohibit all private rights, grazing, and forestry.
- Boundaries: Sanctuary boundaries are not strictly fixed by state legislation and can be modified easily; National Park boundaries are fixed by state legislative enactments.
- Upgradation: A Wildlife Sanctuary can be upgraded to a National Park, but a National Park cannot be downgraded to a Sanctuary.

(ii) Biosphere Reserves: MAB Program, 3 Zonal Structure & Global Conservation Impact

• Biosphere Reserves (UNESCO MAB Program): Initiated in 1971 by UNESCO under the Man and Biosphere (MAB) Program. Biosphere Reserves are multi-purpose protected areas intended to conserve representative biomes and genetic diversity while promoting sustainable socio-economic development of indigenous human communities.

• 3 Zonal Structure of Biosphere Reserves:
- 1. Core Zone: Innermost strictly protected undisturbed natural ecosystem. Legally protected under Wildlife Act. Zero human settlement, grazing, or forestry allowed. Only non-destructive research and monitoring permitted.
- 2. Buffer Zone: Concentric zone surrounding the Core Zone. Managed for activities compatible with conservation: environmental education, eco-tourism, and controlled research.
- 3. Transition Zone (Cooperation Zone): Outermost flexible peripheral zone where local tribal communities, sustainable agricultural practices, forestry, and human settlements co-exist in harmony with nature.

• 3 Functions of Biosphere Reserves:
- Conservation Function: Preserve genetic resources, species, and biomes.
- Development Function: Promote sustainable economic and human development.
- Logistic Support Function: Provide support for research, monitoring, and environmental education.
