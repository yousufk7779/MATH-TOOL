📖 Quick Glossary & Basic Definitions

Essential Core Concepts & Key Definitions • Unit II: Electrochemistry (JKBOSE / CBSE Official Syllabus - 09 Marks)

1. Electrochemistry: The branch of physical chemistry that deals with the interconversion of electrical energy and chemical energy, and the relationship between electrical potential and spontaneous chemical reactions.
2. Specific Conductivity (κ - Kappa): The conductance of a solution of 1 cm length and 1 cm2 cross-sectional area (i.e. conductance of 1 cm3 of electrolytic solution). Unit: S cm-1 or S m-1.
3. Molar Conductivity (Λm): The conducting power of all the ions produced by dissolving 1 mole of an electrolyte in a given volume of solution: Λm = (κ × 1000) / M. Unit: S cm2 mol-1.
4. Kohlrausch's Law of Independent Migration: At infinite dilution, the limiting molar conductivity of an electrolyte is the sum of the individual limiting molar conductivities of its constituent cations and anions.
5. Standard Electrode Potential (E°): The potential difference developed between a metal electrode and its 1 M ionic solution at 298 K and 1 bar pressure, measured relative to the Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE, E° = 0.00 V).
6. Faraday's Constant (F): The total electrical charge carried by exactly 1 mole of electrons: F = NA × e = (6.022 × 1023) × (1.602 × 10-19 C) ≈ 96500 C mol-1.

1. Redox Reactions & Electrochemical Fundamentals

Electrochemistry deals with chemical transformations brought about by electrical energy and the generation of electrical energy by spontaneous chemical reactions. All electrochemical processes are fundamentally based on Redox (Reduction-Oxidation) reactions where electron transfer occurs between interacting chemical species.

  • • Oxidation: Loss of electrons, increase in oxidation state. Occurs strictly at the Anode.
  • • Reduction: Gain of electrons, decrease in oxidation state. Occurs strictly at the Cathode.
  • • Anode (Electrochemical / Galvanic Cell): Negative terminal where oxidation takes place.
  • • Cathode (Electrochemical / Galvanic Cell): Positive terminal where reduction takes place.
💡 REAL-WORLD INTUITION & ANALOGY: Electron Highway
Think of an electrochemical cell as a power highway: electrons are released at the Anode (the power generation station) and travel through the external wire to the Cathode (the consumer station), providing continuous electrical current.

2. Conductance in Electrolytic Solutions & Cell Constant

Unlike metallic conductors where electrical charge is carried by free electrons without any mass transfer, electrolytic conductors carry electrical current through the physical migration of hydrated cations and anions towards oppositely charged electrodes.

(i) Electrical Resistance (R), Conductance (G) & Resistivity (ρ)

  • • Ohm's Law in Solutions: Current flowing through an electrolytic solution is directly proportional to the applied potential difference: V = I R.
  • • Conductance (G): The ease with which electric current flows through the solution. Reciprocal of resistance:
    G = 1 R
    Unit: Siemens (S) or Ω-1 (mho).
  • • Resistivity (ρ - Specific Resistance): Resistance of a solution of unit length (l = 1 cm) and unit cross-sectional area (A = 1 cm2):
    R = ρ l A ⇒ ρ = R A l
    Unit: Ω cm or Ω m.

(ii) Specific Conductivity (κ - Kappa) & Cell Constant (G*)

📌 DEFINITION: Specific Conductivity (κ)
Specific Conductivity (κ): The reciprocal of resistivity (ρ), defined as the conductance of a solution enclosed between two parallel electrodes of unit area of cross-section (1 cm2) separated by a unit distance (1 cm):
κ = 1 ρ = 1 R × l A = G × G*
where G* = l / A is the Cell Constant.

• Cell Constant Formulation:
G* = l A = R × κ = κ G
• Units: Cell constant unit is cm-1 or m-1. Specific conductivity unit is S cm-1 or S m-1 (1 S m-1 = 0.01 S cm-1).

(iii) Molar Conductivity (Λm) & Equivalent Conductivity (Λeq)

📌 DEFINITION: Molar Conductivity (Λm)
Molar Conductivity (Λm): The conducting power of all the ions produced by dissolving 1 mole of an electrolyte in a solution of volume V placed between two large electrodes unit distance apart:
Λm = κ × V = κ × 1000 M
where M is the Molarity (mol L-1) of the solution and κ is in S cm-1.

• In SI Units (κ in S m-1, C in mol m-3):
Λm = κ 1000 × C
• Equivalent Conductivity (Λeq):
Λeq = κ × 1000 N = Λm n-factor
where N is the Normality of the solution.
📝 WORKED BOARD NUMERICAL EXAMPLE: Conductivity and Molar Conductivity Calculation
Problem: Resistance of a conductivity cell filled with 0.1 mol L-1 KCl solution is 100 Ω. If the resistance of the same cell filled with 0.02 mol L-1 KCl solution is 520 Ω, calculate the cell constant, conductivity, and molar conductivity of 0.02 mol L-1 KCl solution. (Conductivity of 0.1 mol L-1 KCl = 1.29 × 10-2 S cm-1).
Step-by-Step Solution:
Step 1 (Cell Constant G*): G* = R × κ = 100 Ω × (1.29 × 10-2 S cm-1) = 1.29 cm-1.
Step 2 (Conductivity of 0.02 M KCl): κ = G* / R = 1.29 cm-1 / 520 Ω = 0.248 × 10-2 S cm-1 (2.48 × 10-3 S cm-1).
Step 3 (Molar Conductivity Λm): Λm = (κ × 1000) / M = (2.48 × 10-3 × 1000) / 0.02 = 124 S cm2 mol-1.

3. Variation of Conductivity & Molar Conductivity with Concentration

1. Variation of Specific Conductivity (κ) with Dilution
Specific conductivity (κ) always decreases with decrease in concentration (dilution) for both strong and weak electrolytes.

• Reason: κ is the conductance of 1 cm3 of solution. On dilution, the number of current-carrying ions present per unit volume (per cm3) decreases, leading to a direct decrease in specific conductivity.
2. Variation of Molar Conductivity (Λm) with Dilution
Molar conductivity (Λm) always increases with dilution (Λm = κ × V).

• Reason: Although specific conductivity κ decreases on dilution, the total volume V containing 1 mole of electrolyte increases much more drastically. The increase in volume more than compensates for the decrease in κ.

(i) Strong vs Weak Electrolytes (Debye-Hückel-Onsager Equation)

  • • Strong Electrolytes (e.g. KCl, NaCl, HCl): Completely dissociated at all concentrations. Molar conductivity increases slowly and linearly with dilution because interionic attractions decrease, allowing ions to move faster.
    Λm = Λm° - A √C
    where Λm° is the Limiting Molar Conductivity at infinite dilution (C → 0) and A is the Onsager slope constant. Λm° is readily obtained by graphical extrapolation to zero concentration.
  • • Weak Electrolytes (e.g. CH3COOH, NH4OH): Have low degree of dissociation (α) at ordinary concentrations. On dilution, α increases steeply (Ostwald's Dilution Law). At infinite dilution, dissociation approaches 100%, causing a steep upward exponential curve.
    ⚠️ Extrapolation Limitation: The curve becomes asymptotic to the y-axis, so Λm° for weak electrolytes cannot be obtained by graphical extrapolation.

4. Kohlrausch's Law of Independent Migration of Ions

📌 DEFINITION: Kohlrausch's Law (1875)
Kohlrausch's Law of Independent Migration of Ions: At infinite dilution, when dissociation is 100% complete and all interionic interactions vanish, each ion makes a definite individual contribution to the total limiting molar conductivity of the electrolyte, irrespective of the chemical nature of the co-ion.

Mathematical Formulation:
Λm° = ν+ λ+° + ν- λ-°
where λ+° and λ-° are the limiting molar conductivities of cation and anion, and ν+, ν- are the stoichiometric number of cations and anions produced per formula unit.
Example: Λm°(Al2(SO4)3) = 2 λ°(Al3+) + 3 λ°(SO42-).

(i) 4 Fundamental Applications of Kohlrausch's Law

1. Calculation of Λm° for Weak Electrolytes: Λm°(CH3COOH) = Λm°(CH3COONa) + Λm°(HCl) - Λm°(NaCl).
2. Determination of Degree of Dissociation (α): α = Λm / Λm° (where Λm is molar conductivity at concentration C).
3. Calculation of Dissociation Constant (Ka): Ka = (C α2) / (1 - α) = [ C (Λm / Λm°)2 ] / [ 1 - (Λm / Λm°) ].
4. Solubility (S) & Solubility Product (Ksp) of Sparingly Soluble Salts: Since saturated solution of AgCl/BaSO4 is extremely dilute, Λm ≈ Λm° ⇒ S = (κ × 1000) / Λm° mol L-1 ⇒ Ksp = S2.
📝 WORKED BOARD NUMERICAL EXAMPLE: Kohlrausch Law Calculation for Acetic Acid
Problem: Calculate Λm° for acetic acid given Λm°(HCl) = 426 S cm2 mol-1, Λm°(NaCl) = 126 S cm2 mol-1, and Λm°(CH3COONa) = 91 S cm2 mol-1. If Λm of 0.001 M acetic acid is 39.05 S cm2 mol-1, calculate its degree of dissociation (α) and Ka.
Step-by-Step Solution:
Step 1 (Λm° calculation): Λm°(CH3COOH) = Λm°(CH3COONa) + Λm°(HCl) - Λm°(NaCl) = 91 + 426 - 126 = 391 S cm2 mol-1.
Step 2 (Degree of dissociation α): α = Λm / Λm° = 39.05 / 391 = 0.10 (10%).
Step 3 (Dissociation constant Ka): Ka = (C α2) / (1 - α) = (0.001 × 0.102) / (1 - 0.10) = (10-5) / 0.9 = 1.11 × 10-5 mol L-1.

5. Electrolysis & Faraday's Quantitative Laws of Electrolysis

Electrolysis is the chemical decomposition of an electrolyte driven by passing direct electrical current (DC) from an external voltage source through its molten state or aqueous solution.

(i) Faraday's First Law of Electrolysis

📌 DEFINITION: Faraday's First Law (1833)
Faraday's First Law: The mass (m) of any chemical substance deposited, liberated, or dissolved at any electrode during electrolysis is directly proportional to the total quantity of electrical charge (Q) passed through the electrolytic solution:
m ∝ Q ⇒ m = Z Q = Z I t
where I = Current in Amperes (A), t = Time in seconds (s), and Z is the Electrochemical Equivalent (ECE).

• Definition of Z: Mass deposited when 1 Coulomb of charge passes (I = 1 A for t = 1 s):
Z = Equivalent Mass (E) Faraday Constant (F) = M n × 96500
where M is Molar Mass, n is number of electrons transferred, and F = 96500 C mol-1.

(ii) Faraday's Second Law of Electrolysis

📌 DEFINITION: Faraday's Second Law (1833)
Faraday's Second Law: When the same quantity of electricity is passed through different electrolytic solutions connected in series, the masses of different substances liberated at the respective electrodes are directly proportional to their equivalent masses (chemical equivalent weights):
m1 m2 = E1 E2 = Z1 Z2

(iii) Products of Electrolysis in Important Systems

1. Molten NaCl (Inert Pt Electrodes):
• Cathode (Reduction): Na+ + e- → Na(s)
• Anode (Oxidation): 2Cl- → Cl2(g) + 2e-
2. Aqueous NaCl (Brine Solution):
• Cathode: 2H2O + 2e- → H2(g) + 2OH- (Higher reduction potential than Na+)
• Anode: 2Cl- → Cl2(g) + 2e- (Preferred over O2 evolution due to oxygen overpotential)
3. Aqueous CuSO4 (Pt Electrodes vs Cu Electrodes):
• Inert Pt: Cathode deposits Cu(s); Anode evolves O2(g) from water oxidation.
• Active Cu Electrodes (Electro-refining): Cathode deposits pure Cu(s); Anode dissolves copper: Cu(s) → Cu2+ + 2e-.

6. Galvanic Cells, Daniell Cell & Standard Electrode Potentials

(i) Daniell Cell & Salt Bridge Functions

The Daniell Cell converts the chemical energy of the spontaneous redox reaction between metallic Zinc and Copper sulfate solution into electrical energy:

Zn(s) + Cu2+(aq) → Zn2+(aq) + Cu(s)   (E°cell = 1.10 V)
  • • Anode Half-Cell (Oxidation): Zn(s) → Zn2+(aq) + 2e-   (E°ox = +0.76 V).
  • • Cathode Half-Cell (Reduction): Cu2+(aq) + 2e- → Cu(s)   (E°red = +0.34 V).
  • • IUPAC Cell Notation: Zn(s) | Zn2+(aq, 1 M) || Cu2+(aq, 1 M) | Cu(s).
  • • Functions of Salt Bridge (Agar-agar paste with inert KCl/KNO3):
    1. Maintains electrical neutrality in both half-cells by allowing ion flow.
    2. Completes the internal electrical circuit.
    3. Prevents liquid-liquid junction potential.

(ii) Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) & Electrochemical Series

📌 DEFINITION: Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)
Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE): The primary reference electrode assigned an arbitrary standard potential of exactly 0.00 V at all temperatures:
Pt(s) | H2(g, 1 bar) | H+(aq, 1 M)   (E° = 0.00 V)
• Half-Reaction: 2H+(aq, 1 M) + 2e- ⇌ H2(g, 1 bar).

• Standard Cell EMF Formulation:
E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode = E°right - E°left

7. Nernst Equation & Applications to Chemical Cells

(i) General Derivation of Nernst Equation

📐 STEP-BY-STEP MATHEMATICAL DERIVATION: Nernst Equation for Electrode Potential and Chemical Cells
For a general reduction half-reaction: Mn+(aq) + n e- → M(s)
From thermodynamics: ΔG = ΔG° + R T ln Q
Since ΔG = -n F E and ΔG° = -n F E°:
-n F E = -n F E° + R T ln [M(s)] [Mn+]
Taking pure solid activity [M(s)] = 1:
E = E° - 2.303 R T n F log 1 [Mn+]
Substituting R = 8.314 J K-1 mol-1, T = 298.15 K, F = 96487 C mol-1:
E = E° - 0.0591 n log 1 [Mn+] = E° + 0.0591 n log [Mn+]

For a complete cell: a A + b B → c C + d D:
Ecell = E°cell - 0.0591 n log [C]c [D]d [A]a [B]b

(ii) Equilibrium Constant (Kc) from Nernst Equation

📌 DEFINITION: Equilibrium Constant (Kc)
When an electrochemical cell reaches chemical equilibrium, cell reaction stops, current ceases, and Ecell = 0. The reaction quotient equals the equilibrium constant (Q = Kc):
0 = E°cell - 0.0591 n log Kc ⇒ log Kc = n E°cell 0.0591

8. Gibbs Free Energy Change (ΔG) & Cell Potential

The electrical work done by an electrochemical cell in 1 second is equal to the product of electrical potential and total electrical charge passed. For maximum reversible work:

Wmax = - ΔG = n F Ecell ⇒ ΔG = - n F Ecell
ΔG° = - n F E°cell = - 2.303 R T log Kc
  • • Spontaneity Criterion: For a cell reaction to be thermodynamically spontaneous, Ecell > 0, which gives ΔG < 0 (negative).
  • • Intensive vs Extensive Property: Ecell is an intensive property (independent of reaction stoichiometry), whereas ΔG is an extensive property (proportional to moles of electrons n).

9. Commercial Batteries: Primary & Secondary Cells

🔋 1. Dry Cell (Leclanché Cell - Primary)
• Anode: Zinc container: Zn(s) → Zn2+ + 2e-
• Cathode: Carbon (graphite) rod surrounded by MnO2 + Carbon powder.
• Electrolyte: Moist paste of NH4Cl + ZnCl2.
• Cathode Reaction: MnO2 + NH4+ + e- → MnO(OH) + NH3.
• Cell Potential: ≈ 1.5 V (Non-rechargeable; NH3 forms complex [Zn(NH3)4]2+ preventing pressure build-up).
🔋 2. Mercury Cell (Primary - Button Cell)
• Anode: Zinc-Mercury amalgam: Zn(Hg) + 2OH- → ZnO(s) + H2O + 2e-
• Cathode: Paste of HgO + Carbon: HgO(s) + H2O + 2e- → Hg(l) + 2OH-
• Overall Reaction: Zn(Hg) + HgO(s) → ZnO(s) + Hg(l).
• Cell Potential: Constant 1.35 V throughout its life (overall reaction contains no ions in solution whose concentration changes).
🔋 3. Lead Storage Battery (Secondary - Automobile Inverter Battery)
• Anode: Spongy Lead (Pb)  |  • Cathode: Lead dioxide (PbO2) grid.
• Electrolyte: 38% (w/w) aqueous H2SO4 (density = 1.30 g mL-1).
• Discharging Reactions:
  Anode: Pb(s) + SO42- → PbSO4(s) + 2e-
  Cathode: PbO2(s) + SO42- + 4H+ + 2e- → PbSO4(s) + 2H2O
  Overall Discharge: Pb(s) + PbO2(s) + 2H2SO4 → 2PbSO4(s) + 2H2O   (E = 2.0 V/cell).
• Recharging Reaction: 2PbSO4(s) + 2H2O → Pb(s) + PbO2(s) + 2H2SO4 (exact reverse).

10. Fuel Cells & Hydrogen Economy

📌 DEFINITION: Fuel Cell
Fuel Cell: A galvanic cell designed to convert the chemical energy of continuous fuel combustion (such as H2, CH4, CH3OH) directly into electrical energy without undergoing thermal intermediate cycles.

• Hydrogen-Oxygen (H2-O2) Fuel Cell (Bacon Cell):
• Anode Reaction: 2H2(g) + 4OH-(aq) → 4H2O(l) + 4e-
• Cathode Reaction: O2(g) + 2H2O(l) + 4e- → 4OH-(aq)
• Overall Reaction: 2H2(g) + O2(g) → 2H2O(l)   (E°cell = 1.23 V).

• Advantages:
1. High thermodynamic efficiency (≈ 70% compared to 40% in conventional thermal plants).
2. Zero harmful emissions (pure water by-product used by Apollo astronauts for drinking).
3. Continuous non-decaying electrical supply as long as fuels are fed.

11. Corrosion & Electrochemical Theory of Rusting

Corrosion is an electrochemical phenomenon where a metal is slowly degraded into its oxide, hydroxide, or sulfide by chemical interaction with atmospheric oxygen and moisture.

📌 DEFINITION: Electrochemical Mechanism of Rusting of Iron
Rusting of iron takes place via localized micro-electrochemical galvanic cells formed on the uneven metal surface in the presence of water containing dissolved CO2/O2:

• Anodic Region (Oxidation of Iron):
2Fe(s) → 2Fe2+(aq) + 4e-   (E° = -0.44 V)
• Cathodic Region (Reduction of Atmospheric Oxygen):
O2(g) + 4H+(aq) + 4e- → 2H2O(l)   (E° = +1.23 V)
• Overall Redox Process:
2Fe(s) + O2(g) + 4H+(aq) → 2Fe2+(aq) + 2H2O(l)   (E°cell = +1.67 V)
The ferrous ions (Fe2+) are further oxidized by atmospheric oxygen to ferric oxide, forming hydrated rust:
4Fe2+(aq) + O2(g) + 4H2O(l) → 2Fe2O3(s) + 8H+(aq)
Fe2O3(s) + x H2O(l) → Fe2O3 • x H2O (Rust)

(i) Methods of Corrosion Prevention

  • • 1. Barrier Protection: Coating the surface with paints, thin oil/grease films, or electroplating with non-corroding metals like Nickel or Chromium.
  • • 2. Sacrificial Protection (Galvanization): Coating iron with metallic Zinc (E°(Zn2+/Zn) = -0.76 V). Because Zinc is more electropositive than iron, it oxidizes sacrificially, protecting the underlying iron even if the coating is scratched.
  • • 3. Cathodic (Electrical) Protection: Connecting underground iron pipes to sacrificial metal blocks (Mg or Zn) by an insulated wire. Mg oxidizes preferentially, forcing the iron pipe to act strictly as a cathode.
  • • 4. Anti-Rust Solutions: Alkaline phosphate or alkaline chromate coatings that form an insoluble passivating protective film and consume H+ ions.

12. Master Revision Formula Cheat Sheet

1. Conductance, Conductivity & Cell Constant: • Conductance: G = 1 / R   [Unit: S or Ω-1].
• Specific Conductivity: κ = (1 / R) × (l / A) = G × G*   [Unit: S cm-1 or S m-1].
• Cell Constant: G* = l / A = R × κ   [Unit: cm-1 or m-1].
• Molar Conductivity: Λm = (κ × 1000) / M   [Unit: S cm2 mol-1].
• Onsager Equation: Λm = Λm° - A √C.

2. Kohlrausch's Law & Ionic Applications: • Limiting Conductivity: Λm° = ν+ λ+° + ν- λ-°.
• Degree of Dissociation: α = Λm / Λm°.
• Dissociation Constant: Ka = (C α2) / (1 - α).
• Sparingly Soluble Salt Solubility: S = (κ × 1000) / Λm° mol L-1  |  Ksp = S2.

3. Electrolysis & Faraday's Constants: • Faraday's 1st Law: m = Z I t = [ (M × I × t) / (n × 96500) ].
• Faraday's 2nd Law: m1 / E1 = m2 / E2.
• Faraday Constant: 1 F = 96500 C mol-1 of electrons.

4. Cell Potential, Nernst Equation & Thermodynamics: • Standard EMF: E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode = E°right - E°left.
• Nernst Equation (298 K): Ecell = E°cell - (0.0591 / n) log Q.
• Equilibrium Constant: log Kc = (n E°cell) / 0.0591.
• Standard Gibbs Energy: ΔG° = - n F E°cell = - 2.303 R T log Kc.
