# Arts & Humanities Subjects — Gold Standard Blueprint & Rules

This rulebook applies to ALL **Arts and Humanities subjects** for Class 11 and Class 12, starting with **Education**, and extending to Political Science, Sociology, History, and Psychology.

---

## 1. Core Pedagogical Philosophy for Arts Students
- **Student Reality:** Unlike Science students who solve numericals and derivations, Arts students study vast theoretical concepts, philosophies, and socio-historical frameworks. They get overwhelmed by bulky walls of text and repetitive questions.
- **Rule of Quality over Quantity:** Never force 100 questions onto an Arts chapter. For a 10-mark board unit, **exactly 25 highly curated, board-guaranteed questions** must be provided.
- **Answer Architecture:** Every answer must follow the board examiner's scoring rubric:
  - **Headings & Subheadings in Theme Color**
  - **Crisp, memorable bullet points** (not unbroken paragraphs)
  - **Bold keywords** for instant recall
  - **No fluff, 100% exam yield**

---

## 2. The 3-Tab Architecture for Arts Chapters

Every chapter in Arts subjects MUST follow the dedicated 3-Tab structure:
- **Tab 1: Reference** — Comprehensive reference textbook guide tailored for humanities.
- **Tab 2: Q & A** — Exactly **25 Board Master Questions** divided into Section A (VSAT), Section B (SAT), and Section C (LAT).
- **Tab 3: MCQs** — Exactly **20 to 25 Smart Interactive MCQs** with live green/red feedback and gamified dashboard.

---

## 3. Tab 1: Reference Theory Standards (Humanities Standard)

1. **Exact Official Syllabus Headings**:
   - Use official syllabus unit codes and titles (e.g., `1.1 Derivation / Etymological meaning of Education`, `1.2 Scope of Education as a discipline`).
   - Subdivide into Roman-numbered subtopics `(i), (ii), (iii)`.
   - **Theme Color Styling:** All subtopic titles, thinker names, and colon-ending terms (e.g., `<b style="color: ${themeColor};">(i) Educare:</b>`) must strictly use the chapter's `themeColor`.

2. **Specialized High-Contrast Educational Cards for Arts**:
   - 🧠 `thinkerQuoteBox`: Official verbatim quotes, thinker portraits/philosophies (e.g. Gandhi's 3 H's — Head, Heart, Hand; Dewey's Pragmatism; Vivekananda's Manifestation).
   - ⚖️ `comparisonTableBox`: Clear side-by-side matrices (e.g., *Narrow vs Broader Meaning*, *Bipolar vs Tripolar Process*, *Western vs Eastern Educational Philosophy*).
   - 💡 `boardExamTipBox`: Scoring strategies showing students how to structure 4-mark and 6-mark answers to secure full marks.
   - 🏛️ `policyConnectBox`: Relevance to modern policies like National Education Policy (NEP 2020) and Right to Education (RTE Act 2009).

3. **Standalone Visual Concept Cards & Flowcharts**:
   - Standalone clean cards displaying concept flowcharts (e.g. *Etymology Tree*, *Agencies of Education*, *Tripolar Triangle*).
   - **Zero Duplicate Titles:** Do not add redundant `📸 Title` on top of diagram cards since the graphic already contains the title.

4. **Master Summary & Glossary**:
   - Quick Glossary at the top (Latin roots, Sanskrit roots, core terminology).
   - Master Revision Mind Map / Key Takeaways card at the end of the overview.

## 4. Tab 2: Solutions Standards — The "Golden 25" Board Model

Every Arts chapter must have **exactly 25 questions**, precisely mapped to the official **80 Marks Board Scheme of Assessment**:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│              EXACT 25 QUESTIONS BREAKDOWN PER CHAPTER                   │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Section A: Very Short Answer (VSAT - 1 & 2 Marks)  → 12 Questions     │
│ • Section B: Short Answer (SAT - 4 Marks)           →  8 Questions     │
│ • Section C: Long Answer (LAT - 6 Marks)            →  5 Questions     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Section A: Very Short Answer (VSAT) — 12 Questions (Q1 to Q12)
- **Marks:** 1 Mark and 2 Marks each.
- **Word Limit:** 20 to 30 words (1 to 2 crisp sentences or bullet items).
- **Focus:** Etymological roots, thinker quotes, direct definitions, one-line distinctions.
- **Purpose:** Fast memorization in under 1 minute per question.

### Section B: Short Answer (SAT) — 8 Questions (Q13 to Q20)
- **Marks:** 4 Marks each.
- **Word Limit:** 80 to 100 words.
- **Structure:** Exactly **4 to 5 bullet points**, each starting with a **bold themed sub-heading**.
- **Purpose:** Train students to write high-scoring answers without rambling.

### Section C: Long Answer (LAT) — 5 Questions (Q21 to Q25)
- **Marks:** 6 Marks each (with board internal choice coverage).
- **Word Limit:** 120 to 150 words.
- **Mandatory 3-Tier Structure:**
  1. `Introduction & Meaning` (2-3 lines setting the philosophical/theoretical context).
  2. `Core Analysis / Key Dimensions` (5-6 comprehensive point-wise headings).
  3. `Conclusion / Educational Implication` (Synthesizing summary).

## 5. Tab 3: MCQs Interactive Quiz Standards (20 to 25 MCQs)

1. **Strict Chapter Syllabus Adherence:**
   - 100% of the questions must be rooted strictly within the prescribed chapter syllabus topics (no arbitrary out-of-syllabus trivia or generic GK).
2. **Easy to Advance Gradual Progression (3 Tiers):**
   - **Tier 1: Basic & Engaging (Easy — Q1 to Q10):** Direct recall, Latin/Sanskrit root terms, word meanings, and direct famous thinker quotes. Builds student confidence immediately.
   - **Tier 2: Conceptual & Understanding (Moderate — Q11 to Q18):** Bipolar vs Tripolar nuances, Narrow vs Broad comparisons, Core philosophies (Gandhi’s 3H / Basic Education, Dewey’s Learning by Doing, Rousseau’s Naturalism).
   - **Tier 3: Analytical & Application (Advance — Q19 to Q25):** Scenario-based pedagogical situations, statement evaluation, thinker comparisons, and board-standard analytical questions.
3. **Format:** Every choice must start with `A):   `, `B):   `, `C):   `, `D):   `.
4. **Balanced Answer Keys:** Options `A`, `B`, `C`, `D` must be evenly distributed.
5. **Interactive Behavior:**
   - **Correct Selection:** Turns **Green** (`#4CAF50`) with **Green Checkmark**; explanation remains hidden.
   - **Wrong Selection:** Selected option turns **Red** (`#F44336`) with **Red Cross**; explanation panel opens showing:
     - `Correct Answer is X)` in bold **Theme Color**.
     - `Explanation:` with concise syllabus-backed reason in pure white (`#FFFFFF`).
6. **Ultra HD Tiered Result Dashboard:** Score tier emojis, live stats (Correct, Wrong, Accuracy), and Retake button.

---

## 6. Tone, Language & Formatting Checklist
- **Language:** Clean, simple, and elegant English. Avoid overly dense academic jargon that discourages students.
- **Typography:** Standard headings (`h2`, `h3`, `h4`), bold text with `themeColor`, zero raw carets (`^`), clean HTML entities.
- **Theme Color Uniformity:** The chapter's primary color must be consistent across overview accents, question numbers, bold terms, and quiz elements.
